Two circuit days on the NSE: what a trader can learn

Tue, 22 Sep 2026 Wed, 23 Sep 2026

We went through every tick-by-tick quote for NSE stocks on two back-to-back days. The questions were simple. Which stocks moved most? How does a stock get stuck at its upper circuit, and can you see it coming? What should you know about listing day and the last 15 minutes of the session?

The short version

Three things worth knowing

Upper circuits
Sellers vanish first

In the minutes before a stock locks at its upper circuit, the sell orders dry up. The buy orders hardly grow until the lock itself. Often the warning comes only seconds before.

Listing day
The band follows the listing price

Two stocks listed on 23 September. Both closed exactly 20% above their listing price, not their issue price. That is where their upper circuit sat.

The last 15 minutes
The last trade is not the close

About a quarter of busy stocks stop trading at 3:15 pm. Their closing price is set in an auction just before 3:30 pm. It can differ from the last trade you saw.

Upper circuits

What happens when a stock hits its upper circuit

The basics

Every NSE stock has a price band for the day, such as ±5%, ±10% or ±20% of the previous close. The price cannot trade outside it. When buyers push the price to the top of the band and no sellers remain, the stock is locked at the upper circuit. Buy orders pile up at that one price. A new buy order joins the back of the queue and is unlikely to be filled.

WHIRLPOOL on 23 Sep: a large stock hits the limit

  1. Until about 1 pm it traded quietly between ₹722 and ₹740.
  2. From 1 pm to 2:54 pm it climbed steadily to about ₹859. Trading volume grew sharply.
  3. At 2:54:12 pm one burst of buying took about 1.7 lakh shares. The price jumped to ₹865.25, its 20% limit. Every sell order was gone.
  4. Sellers came back three times in the next 90 seconds. Each time the price dipped a little and returned to the limit.
  5. From 2:55:40 pm it stayed locked. At the close about 6.8 lakh shares were waiting to buy.

FRONTSP on 23 Sep: a lock in the morning

  1. It traded between ₹1,176 and ₹1,363 in the first hour.
  2. At 10:17:16 am one burst took about 13,700 shares. The price reached ₹1,394.60, its 20% limit, and sellers disappeared.
  3. Sellers returned for about 5 seconds and the price dipped to ₹1,381.30.
  4. From 10:17:46 am it stayed locked until the close, more than 5 hours.

Both stocks allow 5x intraday leverage and have a ±20% band. The two locks on Tuesday looked the same. CUBEXTUB locked at noon after a 10-minute rally. OPTIEMUS jumped from ₹694 to its ₹708.90 limit in two seconds, then locked for good about 30 seconds later.

Warning signs

Before a lock, the sell orders dry up

We took every stock that hit its upper circuit during the day and stayed there for at least a minute. There were 33 such stocks on Tuesday and 45 on Wednesday. For each one we tracked the total sell orders waiting in the order book before the lock. The chart shows the typical stock. A value of 1.0 means as many sell orders as 30 minutes before the lock.

  • The sell side does the moving. The total of buy orders barely changed until the lock itself. On both days it grew by about a fifth at most.
  • Tuesday gave more warning. Five minutes before the lock, the typical stock had lost half its sell orders. Thirty seconds before, only 5% were left.
  • Wednesday gave less. Five minutes before, 82% of sell orders were still there. Thirty seconds before, 36% were left.
  • Lower circuits mirror this. Before a stock locks at its lower circuit, the buy orders dry up while the sell orders stay steady.

Individual stocks fell into three groups on both days:

  1. Slow drain. Sell orders shrank over several minutes. Examples: SAKUMA, DSFCL, KABRAEXTRU and AASTHA.
  2. Sellers gone early. The sell side emptied while the price was still below the limit. Buyers then walked the price up. Example: PEARLPOLY had no sellers 2 minutes before its lock.
  3. No warning. Nothing changed until the last few seconds. One large burst of buying cleared every seller at once. Examples: VISL, PREMIERPOL and FRONTSP.
What this means for you

A sell side that thins out fast near the upper limit is the clearest sign of a coming lock. It often shows up only seconds before, too late to act on by hand. If a stock is already locked, a new buy order is unlikely to be filled.

Bands change

The exchange can narrow a stock's band overnight. On 23 September, GLASSWALL and ALKALI went from ±20% to ±10%. INDOMIM went from ±10% to ±5% and hit its new upper limit the same day. GLASSWALL fell 8.6%. Check a stock's band each morning, not only once.

Listing day

Two new listings, two upper circuits

How listing day works

  • A new listing does not open at 9:15 am. It goes through a special pre-open auction first. Both stocks found their listing price at about 9:41 am.
  • Normal trading in both started at 10:00 am.
  • Before the listing price is found, the quotes on screen are placeholders. We saw bids at 2x the issue price and offers far below it. They are not real trades.

Where the circuit really was

  • Before listing, the stocks carried a very wide band of 0.5x to 2x the issue price. For SS that was ₹212 to ₹848.
  • Once listed, the band became ±20% around the listing price. For SS that was ₹499.20 to ₹748.80.
  • HEROMOTORS hit ₹98.40 at 11:12 am and stayed locked all afternoon. More than 1.3 crore shares were waiting to buy at the close.
  • SS hit ₹748.80 at 1:40 pm. It opened up briefly twice, then stayed locked from 2:12 pm.
What this means for you

On listing day, work out the circuit from the listing price, not the issue price. A screen that still shows the wide pre-listing band will mislead you about how far the stock can run.

The last 15 minutes

Many large stocks go quiet at 3:15 pm

On both days, 210 of the busiest stocks stopped trading from about 3:15 pm until just before 3:30 pm. RELIANCE, SBIN, TITAN, SUNPHARMA and SHRIRAMFIN were among them. This is not a glitch. It is the closing auction.

How the closing auction works

For stocks in the closing auction, normal trading ends at about 3:15 pm. Orders are then collected and matched once, just before 3:30 pm. That single match sets the official closing price. The match came at about 3:28 pm on Tuesday and 3:29 pm on Wednesday.

  • The last trade you saw may not be the close. On Tuesday, FEDDERSHOL's last trade was ₹60.80, but its official close was ₹64.80.
  • Index charts go flat. Nifty drew two almost flat 5-minute candles between 3:15 and 3:25 pm on Tuesday. Many of its stocks were not trading.
  • Volume and quotes look odd during the auction. Some apps may briefly show zero volume, or a best buy price above the best sell price. It settles after the match.
  • Previous close means the official close. The previous close shown the next morning is the auction price, not the last trade.

Glossary

Terms used in this post

Price band (circuit)
The highest and lowest price a stock may trade at today, set by the exchange as a percentage of the previous close.
Upper circuit lock
The price sits at the top of the band and no sell orders remain. Only buy orders are waiting.
Lower circuit lock
The price sits at the bottom of the band and no buy orders remain. Only sell orders are waiting.
Order book
The list of buy and sell orders waiting to be filled, with their prices and quantities.
Issue price
The price at which shares were allotted in the IPO.
Listing price
The price found in the pre-open auction on listing day. It is the base for that day's circuit.
Closing auction
The final few minutes of the session, when orders in some stocks are matched once to set the official close.
Tick size
The smallest step by which a price can change, such as ₹0.05.
Intraday leverage
How many times your margin you can trade within the day. At 5x, ₹10,000 of margin buys ₹50,000 of stock that must be closed the same day.
Turnover
The value of shares traded in a day. ₹10 crore is ₹10,00,00,000.
